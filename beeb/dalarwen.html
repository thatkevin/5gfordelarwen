Dalarwen

Welsh valley enters fourth year with no mobile signal; residents say they are ‘fine’

By Fahima ✔ and Yaz, Bureau of Bars & Coverage • Published 04:00 • Updated 11 times after a complaint
Dalarwen, zoomed out, in a perfect world. The house is in there. So is yours. Bureau graphics

A remote farmhouse on the shore of a mid-Wales reservoir has entered its fourth consecutive year without a single bar of mobile signal, the Bureau can report, and residents insist they are “honestly fine about it.”

The property, known as Dalarwen, sits at the head of the Towy valley beside Llyn Brianne — a reservoir, the Bureau stresses, and not a lake, following representations from a Dr M. Borealis of the University of the Void, who contacted this desk forty-one times.

Readings taken at the property showed 0.000 bars in what one source described as “all four corners of the house, at the same time.” The Bureau is unable to independently verify a fourth corner but has no reason to doubt it.

“We’re not off-grid. We’re under the lid.”

‘Maintained, not natural’

Campaigners for the valley say the silence is not a quirk of the hills but is applied nightly by a body known as the Division of Absence, part of the holdings company ISAMSJ. Approached for comment, the company did not respond, but did enrol this reporter, her editor, and her 41,000 followers as customers, automatically and, it appears, permanently.

One resident, a Mr Lewis, told the Bureau he had thirteen finished videos and no way to upload them. “It’s at one per cent,” he said, holding a phone toward Belgium. “It’s been at one per cent for a while.” He described the situation as “alright, actually.”

The Licence Fee

The Bureau is aware of, and would like to very firmly deny, a persistent theory that a redacted portion of the television licence fee is diverted to the maintenance of this exact silence. A local resident, Nan, said she remained hopeful. “I turned the Wi-Fi off and on again and I feel lovely,” she said. “There is no Wi-Fi.”

The campaign for exactly fifteen 5G towers continues. There is no sixteenth tower. This publication has been asked not to imply that there is.
