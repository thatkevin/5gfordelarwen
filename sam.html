SAM

S. SEDGLEY — the terrestrial half of ISAMSJ, wearing a coat

There are no neighbours for miles at Dalarwen. That is the whole appeal, and it is completely true. And yet the valley has a Sam — on exactly three hectares, behind a leaning gate with a small enamel sign: S. SEDGLEY — 3 ha — DO NOT ANCHOR. Not a neighbour, exactly. Neighbours live somewhere. Nobody is sure whether "Sam" is short for Samuel, or Samantha, or Sedgley, or something longer folded down to fit on a gate. Everyone agrees it is best not to ask Sam directly. Sam is very approachable. That is the problem.

What is known

  • Sam owns the land, the mast rights, the footpaths, and "the space between the bars." If you have ever had no signal here, you have stood on Sam's balance sheet.
  • Sam is the S in ISAMSJ. (The other letters are weather and weaponry. Sam is the part that knocks.)
  • Sam maintains the Exclusion Envelope in the field, by hand, the way a keeper trims a wick — nightly, unhurried, never looking at the sky.
  • The caravans drift toward Sam. They always have. Sam does not pull them. Sam is simply downhill of everything.

Sightings

No one at Dalarwen has ever seen Sam arrive. This is the detail that stays with people.

You see Sam leaving: a figure at the top of the three hectares at dusk, gate swinging, already half into the treeline, one hand raised in a wave that could be hello and is, on reflection, always goodbye. You wave back. By the time your arm is down, the field is empty and exactly three hectares and the grass has rearranged itself, very slightly, into a diagram. Nobody visits Sam. Sam visits.

The three hectares

Pace them and the count comes out different every time; the total comes out the same. Surveyed from the ground: a damp field. Surveyed from the bedroom window at Dalarwen: an org chart, growing in the grass, with a box at the very top that has no name and a dotted line running all the way down to a small box relabelled, in fresh cross-stitch, with yours.

Sam mows it into that shape. Or the shape mows Sam. The direction of the verb has never been established, and Sam, who is very approachable, has never been asked.

If you meet Sam on the hill, be polite. Admire the view. Do not accept the offer of a pitch. Do not, whatever else you do, anchor. Sam will understand completely, and wave, and leave, and you will realise, walking home, that you never once saw which direction Sam came from, and that the wave is still going, somewhere behind you, in the dark, for you.
