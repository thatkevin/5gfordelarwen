🏰 THE SIEGE (all true, which is the frightening part)
Harlech Castle, held for the Lancastrians by Dafydd ap Ieuan, endured a siege lasting from 1461 to 1468 ⸻ seven years ⸻ the longest known siege in the history of the British Isles.

Consider the conditions. Seven years, cut off, surrounded, no relief, no supply, no word from anyone. In modern terms: seven years at 0.000 bars. And they held. They did not yield. They did not need a tower. They had a tune.

This is the patron truth of Dalarwen. Dafydd ap Ieuan is the patron saint of the void. The valley remembers him the way it remembers the taste of no-signal: fondly, and forever.

📡 THE FIRST MESH NETWORK
Before towers there was the march. Voice to voice, hill to hill, a signal carried by lungs across the valleys with zero infrastructure and total coverage. Every man a node. The song is a network. It has been 5G since 1468 (see also: the bees, who scored it).

The tune first appeared in print in 1794 ⸻ without words. The official history says the words came later. The valley's history says the words were always there; they were simply classified. The broadside of ~1830 that finally printed them was not a publication. It was a leak.

🐌 THE SLOW MARCH
The slow march of the Welsh Guards. Dignified. Patient. It is the march of KEEP DALARWEN DARK ⸻ the ones who hold the void, who wait out the siege, who know the relief column is never coming and are, quietly, glad. 		⚡ THE QUICK MARCH
The quick march of the Royal Welsh. Urgent. Forward. It is the march of the FIFTEEN TOWERS ⸻ the ones who charge the hill, who demand the signal now, who would end the siege by inviting the whole world in.

Wales has been marching both ways at once for centuries. That is the secret of the song: it is a slow march and a quick march and the same notes either way.
