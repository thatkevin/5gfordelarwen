What Dalarwen Actually Is
An idyllic, off-grid holiday cottage. Also: an instrument. Also: a lid.

The brochure calls it a former hill-farm homestead on the shore of Llyn Brianne, at the head of the Towy valley. All accurate. It is also the exact point where the valley is thinnest ⸻ where the world wears through, like a jumper at the elbow ⸻ which is why they built a house on it, to hold the elbow shut.

The "no mobile signal" the website advertises is not a feature of the terrain. It is applied. Nightly. The Division of Absence maintains the Dalarwen Exclusion Envelope at exactly 0.000 bars the way a lighthouse keeper trims a wick ⸻ not to keep light out, but to keep something in. You are not off-grid. You are under the lid.

🏠 FIXTURES & FITTINGS (as surveyed)
▪ Solar panels & backup generator. The panels harvest daylight. They also harvest the other thing that falls on a south-facing slope at that latitude, which has no agreed name, which the generator is a backup for.

▪ Water from a private spring. The spring rises from beneath the quiet. It remembers every guest who has washed in it since 1740, and occasionally returns their reflections to the wrong sink.

▪ Five bedrooms, sleeps nine. Three have lake views; the lake views back. Nine is the maximum the house will admit to sleeping. The ninth guest is never on the booking and is always, somehow, already asleep in the single room when you arrive.

▪ Oil Range in the kitchen. Provides all hot water. Runs on something older than oil. The tap gives hot the instant you decide you want it, which is to say slightly before.

▪ Wood-burning stove, large inglenook. See the Cube. It lights easily and counts quietly to fifteen in the embers. Do not ask it what comes after. (Someone did. See above.)

▪ Logs, free, in the lean-to. The pile replenishes overnight. No one delivers them. The lean-to is, technically, an inventory node.

▪ Board-games & jigsaws for rainy days. One box has no title. It plays back. Do not place the sixteenth piece; there isn't one; that's the point.

▪ An electric piano. At 04:00 it plays Men of Harlech, once, slowly, with no one on the stool. This is included in the price.

🌲 THE GROUNDS & NEIGHBOURS
▪ The dam-wall road (Natural Resources Wales; farmers only). It admits you readily. It is less reliable about letting you back out; ask anyone who tried to turn around.

▪ Red kites over the old RSPB reserves. Beautiful. Also the airborne arm of the Silence Auditors ⸻ they circle to confirm the 0.000 reading, daily, from above.

▪ Sam's three hectares, next door, behind the leaning gate marked S. SEDGLEY ⸻ DO NOT ANCHOR. Always exactly three hectares. From the bedroom window the grass resolves into an org chart. Nobody visits Sam. Sam visits.

▪ Miles of empty forest road where you might not meet another soul all day. This is not because it is remote. It is because the roads are Infinite Walks™, and the souls are still on them.

▪ Llandovery, down the valley: castle, chip shop, the Bank of the Black Ox (1799), and a station where a train comes once every four hours and is always four hours away.

One visitor wrote: "It's like entering a time-capsule and leaving civilisation behind."
They were closer to the literal truth than they will ever be permitted to remember.
