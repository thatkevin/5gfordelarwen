The Real Dalarwen
Idyllic, off-grid holiday cottage at Llyn Brianne, Wales

Before the towers, before the Cube, before Lewis wept on the hill ⸻ there is a real place, and it is genuinely magic.

Dalarwen is a traditional Welsh farmhouse on the shores of Llyn Brianne reservoir, tucked into a south-facing hillside at the head of the beautiful Towy valley, near the border of Carmarthenshire and Ceredigion in the foothills of the Cambrian Mountains. Once the homestead of a large hill farm, it's reached along a Natural Resources Wales road that crosses the dam wall and follows the lake ⸻ closed to all but a few local farmers.

It is fully off-grid. No neighbours. No telephone. No mobile signal. The electricity comes from solar panels with a backup generator; the water comes from a private spring. Five bedrooms sleep nine, three with lake views. There's a wood-burning stove in a great inglenook fireplace, a dining room, a conservatory, and a lawn that looks straight out over the water. For rainy days: books, jigsaws, board-games, and an electric piano.

"It's like entering a time-capsule and leaving civilisation behind." ⸻ a real visitor, quoted on the real website.

🏞 THE REAL LOCAL AREA (all true)
▪ Wildlife: red kites over the RSPB reserves at Gwenffwrd & Dinas; otters, pine martens, red squirrels; pied flycatchers, nuthatches, house martins, slow worms. (Yes ⸻ the bees too.)

▪ The land: hanging oak woods, rowan, bog asphodel, wild thyme, ferns and mosses; miles of empty forest road and mountain track where you might not meet another soul all day.

▪ Nearby: wild swimming in the Towy & Cothi valleys, ruined castles, Twm Siôn Cati's cave, the National Botanic Garden of Wales, and the Dolaucothi Roman Gold Mines at Pumsaint. The Brecon Beacons and the Cardiganshire coast (seals and dolphins) are an easy drive.

▪ Llandovery: the historic Drovers' town ⸻ home of the Bank of the Black Ox (1799), a castle, pubs, cafes, a post office, and, crucially, a chip shop.
