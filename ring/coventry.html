Sent to Coventry

To be sent to Coventry is to be spoken to by no one. To be present, and unreachable. To have your messages arrive nowhere, and no reply ever come.

We submit that Coventry is therefore the spiritual capital of the void — a whole city that gave the English language its word for zero bars imposed by choice, centuries before there were bars to lose.

Dalarwen has no signal because of the hills.
Coventry taught the world to want none.

The city, briefly

Lady Godiva, who rode through in protest and asked everyone, politely, not to look — an early and admirable act of going offline. A cathedral bombed to a shell in 1940 and left, deliberately, as ruins beside its successor: a monument to silence kept on purpose. And, later, the birthplace of a certain black-and-white musical movement that told a divided country to, more or less, calm down. A city that knows the value of not saying everything at once.

On the campaign

We watch the fight for fifteen towers with mixed feelings. Signal is connection; connection is good. But some of us have been sent to Coventry so long we have grown to like the quiet, the way one grows to like the quiet. We understand Dalarwen better than most.

(They say even in Peru, high in the Andes, there are places you can stand and reach no one. We find that comforting. We hope you do too.)
