Apian Origins of Fifth-Generation Cellular Technology: A Review

B. Drone1, Q. Regina1, W. Worker1,2 · 1 The Hive, Dalarwen · 2 Department of Waggle Dynamics
Received: the Cretaceous · Accepted: immediately · Peer-reviewed by: the Cube

Abstract

We present evidence that fifth-generation mesh networking was independently developed by Apis mellifera no later than the late Cretaceous. The waggle dance is shown to be a lossless packet-routing protocol; the hive, a self-healing mesh; honey, an incidental data by-product. We argue that the modern "invention" of 5G constitutes, at best, rediscovery, and recommend the immediate return of Band B (colloq. "Bee") to its originators.

1. Introduction

Terrestrial carriers achieve, at Dalarwen, a signal strength of 0.000 bars.[1] A single colony of bees, by contrast, coordinates thousands of foraging nodes across a 3 km radius with zero measured latency and no dropped packets.[2] The disparity is instructive.

2. Methods

Observations were conducted from the conservatory window over one (1) summer. Waggle vectors were recorded; buffering was not observed at any point. A control phone was held toward Belgium and produced no result.[3]

3. Findings

The hive network predates all human telecommunication. Tower 15 of the proposed Dalarwen grid is, notably, tuned to Band B. This is not coincidental (§4).
