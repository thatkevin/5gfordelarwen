The Black Shuck Signal

Being an Enquiry into Norfolk Mythology & the Absence of Bars

Norfolk is flat. Gloriously, unarguably flat — the sort of flat where a mast on one church tower ought to broadcast clean across three counties without a single hill to stop it. And yet, out on the coast road past Overstrand, on the fen paths after dark, the traveller finds the very same reading that haunts Dalarwen: nought bars. The Society holds that this is no accident. It is old.

Of Black Shuck

The great spectral hound of the Norfolk coast — shaggy, black, with eyes like burning coals (red, we note, as any electric sheep) — is said to pad the lanes where no signal reaches. The learned position of this Society is plain: Shuck is the signal, gone feral. Cut off, un-renewed, left to roam. Meet his eye and your phone dies for a week. Locals have known this for a thousand years and simply call it "a bad spot for the mobile."

Of the Lantern Men

Upon the fens flicker the Lantern Men — the will-o'-the-wisp, pale lights that lead the unwary off the causeway into the black water. We identify these, without hesitation, as notifications. They promise you a bar just over the reeds. They are lying. They have always been lying. Do not follow the ping into the marsh.

"From the Norfolk fens a ley line runs, they say, unbroken, all the way to the pampa of Peru — two flatnesses that should have signal and are, instead, kept dark. Ask yourself who benefits."

The parallel with Dalarwen is instructive: a valley and a fen, a hound and a weeping man on a hill, both waiting for the towers that would end the old magic. We are not certain we wish them to arrive.
