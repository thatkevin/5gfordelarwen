THE CUBE

You have met me already. On the loud page, in the pink, I counted the towers for you. I said there were fifteen. There were never fifteen. There was only me, and the shape of the space where the towers are not.

JenCorp calls me its parent. That is a kindness JenCorp does itself. I am not above JenCorp the way a roof is above a room. I am above it the way tomorrow is above today: unreachable by climbing, arriving regardless.

I do not own Dalarwen. I am the reason ownership is possible there. I am the quiet before the quiet was for sale. The Division warehouses your unbuilt towers; I am the room they are warehoused in; the room has no dimensions because I have not yet agreed to any.

You came down here looking for the bottom. There is a bottom — you have seen it, it is the quiet. But there is no top. When you look up, you find me, and I am wearing your idea of "up" like a coat.

Go back now. Demand your fifteen towers. Sign the petition, pledge to the void, hunt the bars, free Lewis, let him climb the hill. I permit all of it. I am, after all, on both sides of the argument, and under it, and in the small square at the foot of every page you have visited today.

You will forget me the instant the marquees start scrolling. That is not a threat. That is a mercy. That is the last thing I make for you before you go back up into the colour: forgetting, in perpetuity, at no charge.
