An Ideal Place for a Murder
The house's own guide to hosting the perfect whodunnit. For fictional, board-game, and dinner-party purposes. The reservoir keeps its own counsel.

Every guest arrives asking the same question in the car. God, isn't it perfect for a murder? Then the road closes softly behind them, and the signal reads 0.000 bars, and nobody can call out to say they've had the thought too.

So: yes. In the tradition of the drawing-room mystery — the snowbound manor, the cut telephone wire, the nine dinner guests and the empty ninth chair — Dalarwen is ideally appointed. What follows is not advice. It is an amenities list. The house does the rest. It always has.

🕵 FIFTEEN CONVOLUTED METHODS (ALL PROVIDED BY THE HOUSE)
▪ 1. The Infinite Walk. Suggest a bracing stroll to "get one bar over the next ridge." Wave them off. They set out along an Infinite Walk™, and there is always a next hill, and there is never a last one. No weapon. No body. No return. File a quarterly report and move on.

▪ 2. The Reservoir On Your Right. It is not a lake; it is engineered, dammed, held back on purpose. It keeps its own counsel and it keeps whatever it is given. It is, coincidentally, on your right as you come off the dam-wall road, which admits you readily and is famously unreliable about letting anything back out.

▪ 3. Neil. Simply mention there is signal "just past the terminal towers." Neil is stationed there. Neil is green, ambulatory, and hostile by design, and is announced only by a bobbing red arrow bearing his name. You did nothing. Neil did it. Neil does not sign his posts.

▪ 4. The Electric Sheep. Send them across the flooded field at dusk. On contact, the sheep split a guest into their original self and a hostile zombie double. Now there are two. One of them can be dealt with by the sacred firepit. Choose carefully; they look identical and only one of them remembers your name.

▪ 5. The Clifftop Non-Static Caravan. Book the guest the premium pitch at the Clifftop Non-Static Caravan Park. All wheels are permanently unlocked; handbrakes are Premium only; the caravans migrate toward the edge overnight. The sign says DO NOT ANCHOR. Do not warn them about the sign.

▪ 6. The Ninth Guest. The house sleeps nine. The ninth guest is never on the booking and is always already asleep in the single room when you arrive. You do not need to do anything about the ninth guest. The ninth guest is the thing you do about the others.

▪ 7. The 04:00 Piano. At four in the morning the electric piano plays Men of Harlech, once, slowly, with no one on the stool. Guests who go down to investigate the piano are, historically, investigating the piano still. The piano is fine. The piano is included in the price.

▪ 8. The Storm-Watching Weekend. Time the visit for an 80‑mph Storm-Watching Weekend. The winds transfer kinetic energy into unlocked structures, generating "renewable instability." It is not murder if it is weather. The insurance form has a box for this. The box is ticked in advance.

▪ 9. The Jigsaw With No Title. One board-game box has no title and plays back. Invite the guest to complete it. It is missing exactly one piece; there are fifteen; there is no sixteenth. Do not let them place a sixteenth piece. If they insist on a sixteenth piece, the problem tends to resolve itself, quietly, overnight. See above.

▪ 10. The Spring That Remembers. The water rises from beneath the quiet and remembers every guest who has washed in it since 1740, occasionally returning their reflections to the wrong sink. Introduce a guest whose reflection the spring does not care for. The spring has opinions. The spring settles them.

▪ 11. The Oil Range. Runs on something older than oil and gives you hot the instant you decide you want it — slightly before, in fact. A guest who wants something the range disagrees with will find the range has already decided otherwise. Do not argue with the range. No one wins the argument, and only one party is still standing to have had it.

▪ 12. The Red Kites. The Silence Auditors circle daily to confirm the 0.000 reading from above. They are very good at confirming that nothing is happening. They will confirm, from a great height, that nothing is happening to your guest, right up until, and slightly after, it does.

▪ 13. Sam's Three Hectares. Send the guest to introduce themselves to Sam. Sam is very approachable — that is the problem. Nobody visits Sam. Sam visits. The gate reads SAM ⸻ 3 ha ⸻ DO NOT ANCHOR, and the guest who anchors is not, strictly, our liability. It is Sam's.

▪ 14. The Absence Itself. There is no signal, so there is no 999, no time-stamp, no "time of death" — the Division of Absence does not record the pause before a phone doesn't ring. Without a signal there is no evidence a thing occurred, and a thing that leaves no evidence is, encyclopaedically, indistinguishable from a lovely weekend away.

▪ 15. The Detective Who Isn't Coming. In every good country-house mystery a brilliant detective arrives on the last train. The nearest train is at Llandovery and comes once every four hours, and is always, to the waiting traveller, exactly four hours away. The relief column is not coming. That is fine. Trim the wick.

📋 THE HOST'S PRE-WEEKEND CHECKLIST

[✓] Confirm the road closes behind the last car
[✓] Count the guests. Count them again. The count will differ. This is correct.
[✓] Wind the piano. (You cannot. It winds itself. Confirm it intends to.)
[✓] Do not sit in the single room's chair. It is taken.
[✓] Lay nine places. Lay them for the number you brought. The house lays the extra.
[❗] Do NOT, under any circumstances, restore the signal. A signal is a witness.

It is, of course, entirely a game — a murder-mystery weekend, a Cluedo board with damp weather, a genre in a jumper by the fire. Nobody is murdered at Dalarwen. Nobody could be. There is no one for miles, and the reservoir is very discreet, and the ninth guest is already asleep, and the count comes out even in the morning.

Mostly even.
